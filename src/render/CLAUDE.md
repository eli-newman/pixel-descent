# src/render

Draws the current state into RGBA framebuffers: the HUD and menus into the 180x320 frame, the slope into a buffer that can run past it (`SceneRenderer`'s `top`). Sees the game only through the read-only `GameView` and `SimView` in `state/view.ts`. White on black, plus a yellow UI accent and two greys used only for the shadow of the board's track: `GREY` on the snow and a softer `SOFT_GREY` inside a side slip. The colour setting (`Settings.color`) swaps in a daytime palette for the slope and the UI (`ui`, `uiEdge`, `uiHot`, `uiHotEdge`, `uiHotSmall`, `uiHotShadow`: white and yellow on the black slope, dark ink and yellow on the white one). Nothing in the UI sits on a box.

## Rules

- May import `../sim`, `../state` and `../core`. No DOM here (`platform/shareDom.ts` does the canvas export).
- Only `framebuffer.ts` writes pixels directly.
- Do not import `state/game` or `sim/sim`, even as types (boundary lint). Writing to a view is a compile error. If you need a new value, add it to `GameView`.
- Lint enforces read-only access here: no `as` casts (except `as const`), type assertions, `!` or ts-comments, and every parameter must be read-only. Only `Framebuffer`, `Mask` and `Rng` may be taken as mutable. Callback parameters with inferred types are skipped, as they take their type from the view. TypeScript still lets a view object be assigned to a local or array annotated with a mutable type, and lint does not catch that, so do not write such annotations.
- The board length and rider scale live in `core/config.ts` (`BOARD_LEN`, `RIDER_SCALE`), because the sim's spin pivot uses them too.
- Visual changes must be checked in the running game (`npm run dev`), not just by tests. Keep `tests/contrast.test.ts` green.
- Button positions come from `state/menu.ts`, do not duplicate them.
- The track is cut through powder, so its edges are broken by spilled grains even on a straight. Every grain must be decided by recorded positions or world pixels, never by the screen or the smoothing of unsettled points, or it shimmers.
- Judge the track with real keyboard steering, not only gentle curves: key presses turn much tighter than a demo does. Keep it calm: earlier versions with a crease, inner wall and heavy powder looked glitchy in real play.
- The track is cut into the snow, so its shadow falls from the wall nearest the sun (the left), matching the trees, whose shadows fall to the right. Do not move it to the far side: that reads as a raised ridge.
- Kickers must read as jumps at a glance, on a phone, the moment they scroll in: straight outline, downhill arrows (cliffs use upward warning chevrons), white only (in colour: packed snow lit from the left, with orange marker poles at the lip corners). A kicker in a jump line adds its pips and nothing else, so it is still the same kicker. Do not draw them with yellow (the UI accent) or with the checker and stipple textures that rocks, bushes and drifts use.
- Visual language of the slope, so a player can tell what things do: whatever stands up casts the checker shadow, and solid white standing things crash you (in colour: the dark green, grey and brown ones). A bush is three round puffs with leaf holes (it slows), never a smooth dome like a rock. Pebbles (`isPebble` in `sim/collision.ts`, the same rule that makes them harmless) are drawn flat with no shadow. Drifts and stars lie on the snow and cast none. Stars are the only five-point star shape. Keep new objects inside this language, and do not make anything look like a kicker (an outlined ramp with downhill arrows). `tests/objects.test.ts` checks it.
- The skier's skis start on the track head like the board's tail, so the ski track lines up with the skis. Board looks carry over to skis: stripes and dots along the outside edge, twin tip curls both ends.
- Colour mode is a look only: it never changes the sim, hitboxes or shadows. Colour sprites reuse the black and white shapes and must cover every one of their pixels (`tests/color.test.ts`). Draw world colours from the palette, never a literal, and add any new colour to `C` (the contrast test rejects anything else on a colour screen). Snow on a sprite is the slope's white, so edge it where it meets open snow or the shape melts away.
- The greys are for the track shadow only. Trees and rocks still use the checker shadow, and `tests/contrast.test.ts` keeps grey off the menus.

## Keeping docs in sync (mandatory)

When you change anything in this folder (add, remove, rename a file, change an exported function, constant or rule), update this file in the same change. Update the root `CLAUDE.md` too if the layout or dependency rules move, and `README.md` if player-facing behavior or paths change.
